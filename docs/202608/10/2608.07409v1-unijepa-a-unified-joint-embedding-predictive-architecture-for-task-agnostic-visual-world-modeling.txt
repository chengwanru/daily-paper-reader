# UniJEPA: A Unified Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture for Task-Agnostic Visual World Modeling 

An Lanji 1 Dawei Liu 1 Jin Li 1 Haoran Xu 1 Mei Chen 1 Yu Tian 1

# Abstract 

Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) have emerged as a principled framework for self-supervised learning of world models in compact latent spaces, yet existing methods are fragmented: some predict masked parts of a single image in latent space (I-JEPA), others learn to predict global photometric transformations (Image World Models), while video-scale JEPAs predict future temporal states and are post-trained for action-conditioned planning (V-JEPA 2, DINO-World, DINO-WM). These objectives are treated as distinct recipes with separate encoders, predictors, and anti-collapse regularizers, hinder-ing a single model from unifying image-level and video-level world modeling. We present 

UniJEPA , a unified JEPA that jointly learns 

photometric prediction (image-level transfor-mations) and temporal prediction (video-level next-state dynamics) in one shared latent space. A single end-to-end objective, composed of a next-embedding prediction loss and a Gaussian regularizer, yields a provably anti-collapse encoder-predictor pair trainable from raw pixels without EMA, stop-gradient, or pre-trained encoders. We show that the same latent space supports controllable abstraction: photometric prediction learns invariant structure while temporal prediction learns equivariant dynamics. After action-conditioned post-training on offline trajectories, UniJEPA enables zero-shot planning by treating goal features as prediction targets. On image, video, and control benchmarks, UniJEPA matches or surpasses task-specific JEPAs while requiring a single loss hyperparameter, and plans up to tens of times faster than generative world models at comparable accuracy.

# 1. Introduction 

A central goal of artificial intelligence is to build agents that learn an internal model of the world by observation and use it to understand, predict, and plan in novel situations (LeCun, 2022). World models formalize this by learning to predict the consequence of an action or the evolution of a scene, either in input space (Ha & Schmidhuber, 2018; Brooks et al., 2024) or in latent space (Hafner et al., 2019; 2023; Assran et al., 2023). Among latent approaches, the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) (LeCun, 2022; Assran et al., 2023) has become a promising self-supervised framework: an encoder maps observations to a compact latent space, and a predictor models dynamics by forecasting the latent representation of future or masked content, without reconstructing raw pixels. Because JEPA discards predictable, task-irrelevant details, it is both data-and compute-efficient relative to generative world models. Yet the JEPA family is fragmented. Image-level JEPAs such as I-JEPA (Assran et al., 2023) predict masked regions of a single image; Image World Models (IWM) (Garrido et al., 2024) generalize this to global photometric transformations, learning world models that can “undo” brightness, contrast, and hue corruptions in latent space. Video-scale JEPAs such as V-JEPA 2 (Assran et al., 2025) and DINO-World (Baldas-sarre et al., 2025) predict future temporal states from large-scale video, and can be post-trained into action-conditioned models (V-JEPA 2-AC, DINO-WM (Zhou et al., 2024)) for zero-shot planning. Meanwhile, LeWorldModel (Maes et al., 2026) shows that a JEPA can be trained stably end-to-end from pixels with a single Gaussian regularizer, avoiding collapse without heuristics. These developments are valuable but isolated: each recipe trains its own encoder and predictor under a bespoke objec-tive, so the photometric and temporal world models live in incompatible latent spaces and cannot be composed. We argue that image-level and video-level prediction are two views of the same world model, and that a single latent space should support both. This unification matters practically: a world model that can both explain how a scene changes under global transformations and predict how it evolves over time is more transferable, more sample-efficient, and can serve a wider range of downstream tasks—from robust 1arXiv:2608.07409v1 [cs.CV] 7 Aug 2026 UniJEPA: Unified Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture 

representation learning to embodied planning. We present UniJEPA , a unified JEPA that jointly optimizes photometric and temporal prediction in a shared latent space with a single collapse-free objective. Our contributions are: • Unified objective. We cast photometric prediction (image-level transformations) and temporal prediction (video-level next-state dynamics) as two instances of one latent prediction task, optimized end-to-end with a single next-embedding prediction loss plus a Gaussian regularizer. Only one loss hyperparameter is needed, in the spirit of LeWorldModel (Maes et al., 2026). • Provable anti-collapse. We give a theorem showing the regularizer prevents representational collapse and yields a well-behaved latent distribution, without EMA, stop-gradient, or pre-trained encoders. • Controllable abstraction. We show photometric pre-diction learns invariant structure (as in contrastive methods (Chen et al., 2020; Grill et al., 2020)) while temporal prediction learns equivariant dynamics (as in masked image modeling (He et al., 2022)), and that interpolating between the two objectives controls the abstraction level of the representation. • Zero-shot planning. After action-conditioned post-training on offline trajectories, UniJEPA enables model-predictive-control planning that reaches visual goals without expert demonstrations or reward signals, con-sistent with DINO-WM (Zhou et al., 2024) but from a unified, end-to-end encoder. Figure 1 sketches the motivation: existing JEPA variants occupy separate latent spaces specialized for image-level, video-level, or action-conditioned prediction, whereas Uni-JEPA funnels all three into a single shared latent space. We evaluate UniJEPA on image (ImageNet), video (Something-Something-v2 (Goyal et al., 2017), Epic-Kitchens (Damen et al., 2018)), and control benchmarks. UniJEPA matches or surpasses task-specific JEPAs and generative world models while training end-to-end with a single hyperparameter, and plans up to tens of times faster than pixel-based generative world models (Brooks et al., 2024; Blattmann et al., 2023) at comparable or better accuracy. 

# 2. Related Work 

Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures. JEPA was proposed as a path toward autonomous machine intelligence (LeCun, 2022). I-JEPA (Assran et al., 2023) learns to predict missing blocks of an image in latent space, and V-JEPA (Bardes et al., 2024) extends this to video with temporal masking. The core design choice is that prediction happens

in a learned, abstract latent space rather than raw pixels. This contrasts with contrastive methods (Chen et al., 2020; Grill et al., 2020; Caron et al., 2021) and masked image modeling (He et al., 2022; Bao et al., 2022), which differ in whether they use negative pairs or reconstruction. data2vec (Baevski et al., 2022) generalizes masked prediction across modalities. Our work builds on this line by unifying image-and video-level JEPA objectives in a single model. 

Image World Models. IWM (Garrido et al., 2024) gener-alizes JEPA prediction from masking to global photometric transformations, treating the predictor as a latent image world model that predicts the effect of an “action” (e.g., brightness change) on the representation. This framing connects self-supervised learning to reinforcement-learning world models and reveals that the predictor’s conditioning controls whether the representation is invariant or equivari-ant. Our photometric prediction head adopts this view, but we couple it with a temporal head rather than treating it in isolation. 

Video world models and planning. World models trained from video enable prediction and control (Ha & Schmidhu-ber, 2018; Hafner et al., 2019; 2023; Hansen et al., 2022). Recent large-scale generative video models can serve as world simulators (Brooks et al., 2024; Blattmann et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2024), but their pixel-space predictions are com-putationally expensive and encode task-irrelevant detail, and denoising-diffusion generators (Ho et al., 2020) are espe-cially costly for online planning. Latent video JEPAs such as V-JEPA 2 (Assran et al., 2025) avoid this by predicting in latent space, and DINO-World (Baldassarre et al., 2025) / DINO-WM (Zhou et al., 2024) exploit a frozen DINOv2 encoder (Oquab et al., 2024) so that the predictor can be trained separately and post-tuned on actions for zero-shot planning. Our unified model retains the planning benefits while learning the encoder end-to-end. 2UniJEPA: Unified Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture 

Anti-collapse regularization. A central difficulty in JEPA training is representation collapse, where the encoder maps all inputs to identical embeddings. Contrastive methods use negatives (Chen et al., 2020); BYOL uses a momentum target (Grill et al., 2020); equivariant SSL uses orthogonal-ization (Garrido et al., 2023). LeWorldModel (Maes et al., 2026) shows that a Gaussian-distributed latent regularizer suffices to prevent collapse end-to-end. We adopt and ex-tend this regularization to the unified setting, and prove its anti-collapse guarantee. 

Backbone architectures. Our encoder is a Vision Trans-former (Dosovitskiy et al., 2021; Vaswani et al., 2017), which provides the spatial patch tokens used for both pho-tometric and temporal prediction. Hierarchical ViTs (Liu et al., 2021) offer an alternative with locality bias, and we find the standard ViT sufficient for our unified objective. For dense-prediction transfer we rely on standard decoder heads (Badrinarayanan et al., 2017; Miao et al., 2021). 

Positioning. Whereas prior JEPA methods specialize in either photometric prediction (Garrido et al., 2024) or tempo-ral prediction (Assran et al., 2025; Baldassarre et al., 2025), UniJEPA trains both in one shared latent space with a single objective, provably anti-collapse, and demonstrates that the resulting representation supports both robust image learning and zero-shot action-conditioned planning. To our knowl-edge this is the first unified JEPA coupling image-level and video-level world modeling in a single end-to-end latent space.

3.4. Controllable Abstraction 

The two objectives induce complementary inductive biases. Photometric prediction, which maps x and τ (x) through the same encoder and predicts across them, encourages the representation to be invariant to photometric nuisances. Temporal prediction, which predicts the future state, encour-ages the representation to be equivariant to the temporal dynamics. By weighting the two losses, we can interpolate between these regimes: 

Proposition 3.2 (Invariance–equivariance spectrum) . As 

Lphoto dominates, the representation becomes invariant to the photometric group P, i.e., fθ (τ (x)) ≈ fθ (x). As Ltemp 

dominates, the representation becomes equivariant to the transition operator: gψ (zt, a t) ≈ fθ (xt+1 ).

This gives a practical knob to control the abstraction level of the learned representation, consistent with the observation that JEPAs can span invariance and equivariance (Garrido et al., 2024).

3.6. Scalability 

Because UniJEPA predicts in a compact latent space with a single end-to-end objective, it inherits the efficiency of JEPA world models: no pixel reconstruction, no EMA, and a single loss hyperparameter. Training the 15 M-parameter variant on a single GPU completes in a few hours, consis-tent with LeWorldModel (Maes et al., 2026), while planning is up to tens of times faster than generative world models (Brooks et al., 2024; Blattmann et al., 2023). The archi-tecture scales to larger encoders and longer video horizons without changing the objective. 

# 4. Experiments 

4.1. Setup Datasets. For image-level evaluation we use ImageNet-1k linear probing and fine-tuning. For video understanding we use Something-Something-v2 (Goyal et al., 2017) (mo-tion) and Epic-Kitchens-100 (Damen et al., 2018) (action anticipation), together with the Perception Test (Patterson et al., 2024) and TempCompass (Liu et al., 2024) for diag-nostic analysis. For planning we use offline control suites with goal-reaching tasks spanning mazes, push manipulation (Zhao et al., 2023; Agarwal et al., 2022), and multi-particle control (Kalashnikov et al., 2018), following the offline world-model protocol of (Zhou et al., 2023). We report top-1 accuracy, recall-at-K, and planning success rate. 

Baselines. We compare against task-specific JEPAs (I-JEPA (Assran et al., 2023), IWM (Garrido et al., 2024), V-JEPA (Bardes et al., 2024), V-JEPA 2 (Assran et al., 2025), DINO-WM (Zhou et al., 2024), LeWorldModel (Maes et al., 2026)), contrastive methods (SimCLR (Chen et al., 2020), DINOv2 (Oquab et al., 2024)), and generative world models (Brooks et al., 2024; Blattmann et al., 2023).

Latent prediction is physically consistent. When we ap-ply a photometric transformation τ (e.g., increasing contrast) and predict the resulting latent, the nearest-neighbor visu-alizations retrieved from the predicted latent consistently show the expected brightness and hue shift, even though the model never reconstructs pixels. This indicates that the photometric world model has captured the effect of the trans-formation in a way that is semantically meaningful rather than a trivial memorization. 

Planning is goal-directed and smooth. On maze and push tasks, the latent rollouts produce action sequences that move the agent smoothly toward the goal, and the predicted trajectory remains close to the feasible manifold of the en-vironment. Because predictions are made in latent space,

# 5. Conclusion 

We presented UniJEPA, a unified Joint-Embedding Predic-tive Architecture that jointly learns photometric and tempo-ral world models in a single shared latent space, optimized end-to-end with a single anti-collapse objective. We proved the regularizer prevents collapse, showed that the two objec-tives offer a controllable invariance–equivariance spectrum, and demonstrated that the same representation supports ro-bust image learning and zero-shot action-conditioned plan-ning. UniJEPA matches or surpasses task-specific JEPAs and generative world models while being substantially more compute-efficient. 7UniJEPA: Unified Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture

Limitations and future work. Our photometric and tem-poral heads share a predictor; a richer conditioning mecha-nism may be needed for long-horizon or multimodal tasks. The Gaussian regularizer, while provably anti-collapse, may limit representational capacity at extreme scales. Compared with generalist and vision-language-action agents (Reed et al., 2022; Zitkovich et al., 2023; Etukuru et al., 2024), Uni-JEPA currently does not consume language instructions; ex-tending UniJEPA to unified multimodal token spaces (Yang et al., 2026d;c), incorporating topological orthogonality for more faithful tokenization (Yang et al., 2026a), and applying it to interpretable, object-centric manipulation (Yang et al., 2026b) are promising directions. 

# Impact Statement 

This paper develops representation-learning and world-model methods intended to advance efficient, sample-efficient, and interpretable agents. The efficiency gains could reduce the computational footprint of training and planning. As with all world models, the predicted dynam-ics may be imperfect and should be validated before de-ployment in safety-critical or autonomous settings. We encourage careful evaluation of plan reliability before field deployment.

# A. Implementation Details 

We use a ViT-Small/16 encoder ( 15 M parameters) for control and ViT-Large for large-scale image/video representation. The predictor is a lightweight MLP/ViT. All models are trained end-to-end from raw pixels with the Adam optimizer. For photometric prediction we use brightness, contrast, saturation, and hue augmentations. For temporal prediction we use contiguous video clips. The regularizer is applied on a batch of embeddings projected onto 512 random directions. Random seed is fixed for all runs.

# C. Proof of Anti-Collapse 

We provide the full statement and proof of Theorem 3.1. The key is that a degenerate latent distribution makes the χ21

regularizer unbounded. Let Pz be the law of z = fθ (x). If Var( uTz) = 0 for some unit u, then uTz is almost surely constant, so its normalized squared deviation diverges, making R unbounded. Therefore any finite R ≤ ε implies all projections have variance bounded away from zero, which is equivalent to the covariance being uniformly positive definite. The constant-encoder contradiction follows immediately.
